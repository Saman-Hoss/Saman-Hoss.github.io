This project develops data-parsimonious models for assessing the risk of West Nile virus spillover. The main goal is to create modeling frameworks that do not require site-specific entomological surveillance data and can be adapted to different geographic regions.

The approach combines epidemiological case data, environmental drivers, and mechanistic reasoning to estimate long-term spillover risk. A central idea of this project is to build models that are practical for locations where detailed local mosquito surveillance is limited or unavailable.

Methods and tools: compartmental modeling, Bayesian prediction, temperature-dependent mosquito dynamics, risk assessment, Python.

Related work: A Data-Parsimonious Model for Long-Term Risk Assessments of West Nile Virus Spillover.
