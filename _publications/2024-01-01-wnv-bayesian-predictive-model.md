excerpt: "A nonacademic summary: To predict the number of new cases of West Nile virus, we established a minimal model with only two parameters that uses only the time series of human cases in the target year. In fact, the incidence versus cumulative cases curve method was originally developed for a basic SIR model. We extended this approach to a more complex compartmental model of West Nile virus that includes three populations: humans, mosquitoes, and birds. The results of the model predictions for all U.S. states were submitted to the CDC challenge for forecasting the number of monthly cases.

The key feature of this model is that the number of parameters to be estimated is minimal, which reduces the amount of introduced noise in the system. In addition, the model does not require historical data and relies only on data from the beginning of the outbreak to predict its trajectory over time. Therefore, it is not site-specific and can be applied anywhere, as long as initial West Nile virus case data are available."
